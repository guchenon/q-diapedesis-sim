## 1. Methodological & Epistemic Transparency Disclaimer

Although the individual biophysical and mathematical laws on which **Q-DIAPEDESIS** relies—namely in-vivo endothelial surface-layer rheology, selectin catch–slip bond kinetics, ischemic tissue acidosis, Piezo1-mediated shear mechanotransduction, magnetic step-out dynamics in rotating fields, Floquet–Lindblad open-system expansions, and Pennes bioheat transfer—are grounded in peer-reviewed empirical literature (**Status A**), the specific numerical thresholds defining the integrated four-layer architecture represent **investigator-defined a-priori design targets and falsifiable in-silico hypotheses (Status B)**.

Specifically, the $15–50\ \mu\text{m}$ operating envelope ($30\ \mu\text{m}$ default), the $0.50\ \mu\text{m}$ baseline glycocalyx thickness (with $0.4–1.0\ \mu\text{m}$ sensitivity bounds), the $15\text{ pN}$ nominal catch-bond transition (midpoint of $10\text{ pN}$ monomeric and $20\text{ pN}$ dimeric ceilings), the buffered perivascular/luminal target $\text{pH} \ge 7.15–7.35$, the $\tau_{\text{wall}} \le 5.50\text{ Pa}$ total wall-shear ceiling with $\ge 92\%$ modeled structural glycocalyx retention, the $80\text{ Hz}$ and $5\text{ Hz}$ bimodal Floquet drive pair ($\ge 92\%$ dual-task fidelity), the $< 2.0\%$ transient bifurcation washout rate per pass, the $< 8.0\ \mu\text{m}$ PINO tracking error across a $50–200\text{ ms}$ MRI blind window, the $\Delta T < 0.30\ ^\circ\text{C}$ local thermal margin, and the $\ge 99.0\%$ final mass-balance retrieval efficiency are formulated strictly as falsifiable hypotheses. ## 3. Pillar-by-Pillar Verified Literature Synthesis

### Pillar 1: Cerebral Microvascular No-Reflow, Endothelial Glycocalyx & Piezo1 NETosis (Layer 1)
* **Clinical No-Reflow Incidence & Futile Recanalization:** First described in rabbit brain ischemia by **Ames et al. (*Am. J. Pathol.* 1968; PMID: 5635861)**. In modern mechanical thrombectomy (MT), **Rivet et al. (*Stroke* 2025; `10.1161/STROKEAHA.124.049574`)** analyzed 456 pooled EXTEND-IA patients and showed persistent tissue hypoperfusion in $20\%–30\%$ of angiographically recanalized (eTICI 2c–3) patients, reducing 90-day favorable outcomes from $67.5\%$ to $43.3\%$ (adjusted OR $0.31$). **Horie et al. (*Stroke* 2025; `10.1161/STROKEAHA.124.048994`)** detected hyperacute hypoperfusion ($\text{Tmax} > 6\text{ s}$ at $30\text{ min}$) in $80\%$ ($52/65$) of TICI $\ge 2\text{b}$ patients, while **Qiao et al. (*Ann. Neurol.* 2026; `10.1002/ana.78142`)**, **Sperring et al. (*Stroke* 2023; `10.1161/STROKEAHA.123.044240`)**, and **Liu et al. (*Exp. Neurol.* 2026; `10.1016/j.expneurol.2025.115532`)** confirm that macrovascular recanalization fails to guarantee microvascular reperfusion. Furthermore, **Wong et al. (*Stroke* 2021; `10.1161/STROKEAHA.120.033377`)** documented periprocedural distal emboli in $42\%$ ($21/50$) of MT patients on MRI.
* **Penetrating Arteriole Bottlenecks & Cellular Stalling:** **Nishimura et al. (*Nat. Methods* 2006; `10.1038/nmeth844`)**, **Shih et al. (*Nat. Neurosci.* 2013; `10.1038/nn.3278`)**, and **Blinder et al. (*Nat. Neurosci.* 2013; `10.1038/nn.3426`)** proved that penetrating arterioles are non-redundant cortical bottlenecks where occlusion of a single vessel produces focal infarction and cognitive deficits. Downstream obstruction is driven by oxidative-nitrative pericyte constriction (**Yemisci et al., *Nat. Med.* 2009; `10.1038/nm.2022`**; **Hall, Attwell et al., *Nature* 2014; `10.1038/nature13165`**) and hyperacute neutrophil capillary stalling (**Erdener et al., *J. Cereb. Blood Flow Metab.* 2021; `10.1177/0271678X20914179`**; **Barbour et al., *Neurobiol. Dis.* 2026; `10.1016/j.nbd.2026.107460`**).
* **In-Vivo Microvascular Rheology:** **Pries, Neuhaus & Gaehtgens (*Am. J. Physiol.* 1992; `10.1152/ajpheart.1992.263.6.H1770`)**, **Pries, Secomb et al. (*Circ. Res.* 1994; `10.1161/01.RES.75.5.904`)**, and **Pries & Secomb (*Am. J. Physiol. Heart Circ. Physiol.* 2005; `10.1152/ajpheart.00297.2005`)** established the empirical in-vivo Fåhræus–Lindqvist viscosity law incorporating macromolecular endothelial surface layer (ESL) retardation ($W \approx 0.8–1.1\ \mu\text{m}$ effective layer) and microvascular bifurcation phase separation (**Pries et al., *Microvasc. Res.* 1989; `10.1016/0026-2862(89)90018-6`**).
* **Endothelial Glycocalyx Vulnerability:** Structural glycocalyx thickness is $\sim 500\text{ nm}$ ($0.50\ \mu\text{m}$) (**Weinbaum, Tarbell & Damiano, *Annu. Rev. Biomed. Eng.* 2007; `10.1146/annurev.bioeng.9.060906.151959`**; **Dull & Hahn, *Crit. Care* 2022; `10.1186/s13054-022-04154-2`**). During early ischemia-reperfusion, plasma syndecan-1 surges $42$-fold and heparan sulfate $10$-fold within $0–15\text{ min}$ (**Rehm et al., *Circulation* 2007; `10.1161/CIRCULATIONAHA.106.684852`**), with dynamic shedding confirmed in stroke thrombectomy cohorts (**Xu et al., *Front. Neurol.* 2024; `10.3389/fneur.2024.1321909`**; **Günay et al., *Neurol. Int.* 2026; `10.3390/neurolint18050077`**). While laminar physiological shear ($\sim 1\text{ Pa}$) supports glycocalyx biosynthesis, disturbed/oscillatory shear degrades it (**Wang G et al., *ATVB* 2020; `10.1161/ATVBAHA.119.313399`**).
* **NETs Thrombolysis Resistance & Piezo1 Shear-Induced NETosis:** Retrieved stroke thrombi are heavily cross-linked by tPA-resistant Neutrophil Extracellular Traps (NETs) (**Laridan et al., *Ann. Neurol.* 2017; `10.1002/ana.24993`**; **Peña-Martínez et al., *Stroke* 2019; `10.1161/STROKEAHA.119.026848`**). Crucially, **Baratchi et al. (*Nat. Commun.* 2024; `10.1038/s41467-024-51211-1`)** proved that fluid shear stress ($4, 20, \text{and } 80\text{ dyn/cm}^2$, i.e., $0.4, 2.0, \text{and } 8.0\text{ Pa}$) triggers dose-dependent neutrophil NETosis via the mechanosensitive ion channel **Piezo1**.
* **Acidotoxicity & Post-EVT Autoregulation Failure:** Focal ischemia drops extracellular brain $\text{pH}$ to $6.0–6.5$ (**Zha, Xiong & Simon, *J. Cereb. Blood Flow Metab.* 2022; `10.1177/0271678X221089074`**; **Wang et al., *NeuroImage* 2019; `10.1016/j.neuroimage.2019.02.022`**), activating $\text{Ca}^{2+}$-permeable ASIC1a channels (**Xiong et al., *Cell* 2004; `10.1016/j.cell.2004.08.026`**). Simultaneously, exceeding individualized cerebral autoregulation limits post-MT increases hemorrhagic transformation and worsens 90-day outcomes (**Petersen et al., *Stroke* 2020; `10.1161/STROKEAHA.119.026596`**), while aggressive systemic BP lowering ($<120–140\text{ mmHg}$) causes hypoperfusion harm in randomized trials (**ENCHANTED2/MT, Yang et al., *The Lancet* 2022; `10.1016/S0140-6736(22)01882-7`**; **OPTIMAL-BP, Nam et al., *JAMA* 2023; `10.1001/jama.2023.14590`**).

### Pillar 2: Biomimetic Catch-Bond Margination & Hydro-Magnetic Synchronization (Layer 2)
* **Selectin Catch-Bond Kinetics:** **Marshall, Long, Piper, Yago, McEver & Zhu (*Nature* 2003; `10.1038/nature01605`)** and **Barsegov & Thirumalai (*PNAS* 2005; `10.1073/pnas.0406938102`)** demonstrated that P-selectin/PSGL-1 bonds exhibit catch-bond prolongation of bond lifetime up to $\sim 10\text{ pN}$ (monomer) and $\sim 20\text{ pN}$ (dimer) before transitioning to slip dissociation (with L-selectin shear thresholds shown by **Yago et al., *J. Cell Biol.* 2004; `10.1083/jcb.200403144`** and periodic forcing analyzed by **Pereverzev & Prezhdo, *Biophys. J.* 2006; `10.1529/biophysj.106.087288`**).
* **Phase-Lagged Active Rotator Synchronization:** **Sakaguchi & Kuramoto (*Prog. Theor. Phys.* 1986; `10.1143/PTP.76.576`)** formulated phase-lagged synchronization ($\dot{\theta}_i = \omega_i + \frac{K}{N}\sum \sin(\theta_j - \theta_i - \alpha)$), complemented by hydrodynamic rotor synchronization (**Uchida & Golestanian, *Phys. Rev. Lett.* 2010; `10.1103/PhysRevLett.104.178103`**) and coupled oscillator swarming (**O'Keeffe, Hong & Strogatz, *Nat. Commun.* 2017; `10.1038/s41467-017-01190-3`**).

### Pillar 3: Bimodal Step-Out / Floquet Control, Lindblad TTNs & Neural Operators (Layer 3)
* **Step-Out Frequency & Heterogeneous Swarm Addressing:** **Mahoney, Nelson, Peyer, Nelson & Abbott (*Appl. Phys. Lett.* 2014; `10.1063/1.4870768`)** proved that rotating magnetic microrobots with distinct step-out frequencies ($\omega_{\text{so}} = \mu_0 |\mathbf{m}||\mathbf{h}|/c$) can be selectively driven by a single global rotating field. Recent extensions include chiral frequency-band selection (**Giltinan et al., *Appl. Phys. Lett.* 2020; `10.1063/1.5143007`**), size-heterogeneous coupled-oscillator collectives (**Ceron, Gardi, Petersen & Sitti, *PNAS* 2023; `10.1073/pnas.2221913120`**), and heterogeneous microswarm task division (**Cao, Mou, Guan et al., *Adv. Sci.* 2024; `10.1002/advs.202401711`**).
* **Classical Floquet–Magnus & Open Lindblad Formalism:** High-frequency Floquet expansions (**Bukov, D'Alessio & Polkovnikov, *Adv. Phys.* 2015; `10.1080/00018732.2015.1055918`**; **Goldman & Dalibard, *Phys. Rev. X* 2014; `10.1103/PhysRevX.4.031027`**) have been rigorously extended to classical Langevin and stochastic Landau–Lifshitz–Gilbert equations (**Higashikawa, Fujita & Sato, `arXiv:1810.01103`**; **Sato & Ikeda, *J. Phys. Soc. Jpn.* 2025; `10.7566/JPSJ.94.111007`**). Open Markovian dissipation is governed by the GKLS generator (**Lindblad, *Commun. Math. Phys.* 1976; `10.1007/BF01608499`**; **Gorini, Kossakowski & Sudarshan, *J. Math. Phys.* 1976; `10.1063/1.522979`**), subject to stroboscopic existence conditions (**Schnell, Eckardt & Denisov, *Phys. Rev. B* 2020; `10.1103/PhysRevB.101.100301`**).
* **Conditional Absence of Barren Plateaus in Tensor Networks & Equivariant Models:** While generic variational ansätze suffer exponentially vanishing gradients (**McClean et al., *Nat. Commun.* 2018; `10.1038/s41467-018-07090-4`**), polynomial gradient variance ($\Omega(1/\text{poly}(N))$) under local costs and 2-design initializations is proven for Isometric Tree Tensor Networks (**Barthel & Miao, *Commun. Math. Phys.* 2025; `10.1007/s00220-024-05217-x`**; **Cervero Martín, Plekhanov & Lubasch, *Quantum* 2023; `10.22331/q-2023-04-13-974`**), QCNNs (**Pesah, Cerezo et al., *Phys. Rev. X* 2021; `10.1103/PhysRevX.11.041011`**), and $S_n$-Permutation-Equivariant architectures (**Schatzki, Larocca, Nguyen, Sauvage & Cerezo, *npj Quantum Inf.* 2024; `10.1038/s41534-024-00804-1`**; **Nguyen et al., *PRX Quantum* 2024; `10.1103/PRXQuantum.5.020328`**). Crucially, **Cerezo et al. (*Nat. Commun.* 2025; `10.1038/s41467-025-63099-6`)** show that barren-plateau-free subspaces frequently admit efficient classical simulation—directly supporting Q-DIAPEDESIS's classical in-silico execution of the TTN controller.
* **MRI Duty-Cycle Multiplexing & Neural Operators:** Clinical MRI navigation multiplexes tracking ($22\text{ ms}$) and propulsion ($19\text{ ms}$ in a $41\text{ ms}$ / $24\text{ Hz}$ cycle; **Martel et al., *Comput. Aided Surg.* 2008; `10.3109/10929080802551274`**; **Martel et al., *Int. J. Robot. Res.* 2009; `10.1177/0278364908104855`**), while real-time unrolled MRI brain intervention networks exhibit $80\text{ ms}$ 2D frame times and $0.4\text{ s}$ latency (**He, Feng, Yang G-Z et al., *Nat. Commun.* 2023; `10.1038/s41467-023-43966-w`**; **Huang, Yang G-Z et al., *Med-X* 2023; `10.1007/s44258-023-00003-1`**). Fourier and Physics-Informed Neural Operators (**Li, Kovachki, Anandkumar et al., *ICLR* 2021, `arXiv:2010.08895`**; **Li et al., *ACM/IMS J. Data Sci.* 2024; `10.1145/3648506`**) achieve $\sim 5\text{ ms}$ 2D Navier–Stokes inference on GPUs.
